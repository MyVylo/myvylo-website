Yes. Custom categories let you keep a side gig separate from your usual household spending and salary. You can use one income category for the whole gig, or separate different kinds of work.

## An example: weekend photography

| What happened | Category | Where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A client pays $600 for a shoot. | **Photography income**, created inside the Income group. | Income activity shows the payment and your category name. |
| You buy $80 of supplies. | **Photography supplies**, created in an expense group. | Transactions and the category’s spending in Spend. |
| You pay $20 for editing software. | **Photography software**, kept separately from your household subscriptions. | Transactions and the category’s spending in Spend. |

For those three transactions, $600 came in and $100 went out, leaving $500. You can compare the income payments with the expense categories; Vylo doesn’t create a separate business profit report from them.

## Choosing your categories

- **Put earnings under Income.** Names such as Tutoring, Freelance writing, or Market sales describe where the money came from. Putting a category in an expense group won’t make it an income category.
- **Give costs their own expense categories.** You can start with one, such as Side-gig expenses, or [create a group](/help/articles/category-groups/) with Supplies, Software, and Travel underneath it.
- **Assign existing transactions.** New categories start empty. Correct earlier payments and purchases you want included in the breakdown.
- **Remember a merchant only when the choice fits future purchases.** A dedicated software provider may always be a work expense. A shop you use for both home and work may need a separate decision each time.

The [add-a-category guide](/help/articles/add-a-category/) shows the web and iPhone steps. Choose Income for earnings and an expense group for costs. The [transaction category guide](/help/articles/change-transaction-category/) shows how to assign each payment or purchase.

## Where does the income appear?

Open **Income** from Dashboard on the web or Home on iPhone. The month’s **Income activity** shows your custom category beside each payment. On iPhone, choose **Total** to include all income types in the list. [See the income guide](/help/articles/income-basics/).

Income categories don’t each create their own summary card. Use the activity list to check the individual payments and category names.

## Cash payments and payouts after fees

If a customer pays in cash and the payment won’t be imported from a bank, [add it manually](/help/articles/add-a-transaction/) using your income category. Don’t add a second entry for a payment that’s already been imported.

If a platform keeps a fee before paying you, the bank transaction shows the amount you actually received. For example, a $100 sale paid out as $92 imports as $92. Vylo can’t infer the missing $8 fee from that deposit alone.

## Costs someone will reimburse

A purchase your employer or client will pay back is different from a cost you’re covering yourself. You may want both the purchase and repayment outside your household budget. [Read about excluded categories and reimbursements](/help/articles/excluded-categories/).
