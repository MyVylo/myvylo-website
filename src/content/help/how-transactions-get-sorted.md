When a transaction arrives from your bank, Vylo tries to put it in the right category. Most purchases can be sorted using merchant names and built-in rules. AI is an optional extra step.

## Your saved merchant choices come first

If you’ve told Vylo to remember a merchant, it uses that category for future matching purchases. For example, saving FreshCo as Groceries means you shouldn’t have to make the same choice next week.

Changing one transaction and saving a merchant rule are different actions. Use **Remember merchant** on the web, or **Always use this category** in the iPhone review queue, when you want the choice to carry forward.

## Vylo checks the transaction against its rules

Vylo recognizes many merchants and looks for words that help identify a purchase. It also has specific rules for things such as transfers, credit-card payments, bank fees, and income, so moving money between accounts isn’t treated like buying something.

You can [add your own category keywords](/help/articles/category-rules/) and use the category names that make sense to your household.

## AI can help with an unclear merchant—after you agree

For eligible transactions the rules haven’t placed confidently, Vylo can ask OpenAI to suggest a category. **The person who connected the bank must give explicit consent first.** Joining a household or connecting a bank does not grant AI permission.

Vylo sends a shortened merchant name or bank description, with detected identifying details removed. It does not add the amount, date, account balance, or your budget. [See exactly what is sent and how consent works](/help/articles/ai-categorization/).

## Anything that needs a decision goes to review

Open the review queue to check uncertain or uncategorized transactions. Choose a category, confirm it, or skip the transaction until you know what it is. You can also correct a category directly in **Transactions** on the web or **Activity** on iPhone.

A store can sell groceries, gifts, and furniture on the same receipt. Vylo can recognize the store; you still know best what the purchase was for.
